About

I work in applied and computational mathematics, with a focus on methods that remain reliable when models are imperfect, data are sparse or noisy, and uncertainty enters through parameters, forcing, initial conditions, or the solution operator itself. My research brings together numerical PDEs, data assimilation, uncertainty quantification, and scientific machine learning for modeling and prediction of complex physical systems.

My doctoral work at Auburn University focused on domain-decomposition and time-stepping methods for flow, transport, and related PDE models. More recently, I have been developing data-assimilation techniques for state and parameter estimation, together with uncertainty-aware surrogate models, including stochastic and diffusion-based operator networks. My long-term goal is to couple these ideas so that AI-based forecast models can be calibrated with observational data and used reliably for high-dimensional PDE and SPDE systems.

Research interests

Core themes spanning analysis, computation, and data-driven modeling.

  • Numerical analysis & scientific computing

    Structure-preserving discretizations and efficient solvers for PDEs arising in scientific applications.

  • Data assimilation & UQ

    Filtering and uncertainty quantification for PDEs and stochastic PDEs, including joint state–parameter estimation.

  • Scientific machine learning

    Neural operators, operator learning, and diffusion-based models for learning solution maps of deterministic and stochastic systems.

  • Domain decomposition & local time-stepping

    Decoupled, nonconforming, and locally adaptive time-stepping schemes for multiscale problems.

  • Heterogeneous & coupled problems

    Flow and transport in porous and fractured media, reduced fracture models, and related coupled systems.
